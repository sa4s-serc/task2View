Folder 1

Folder 1-GR10-Q1:
The content of the diagram is generally correct. However, I would have expected an additional higher-level decomposition showing how the identified components are distributed across the main architectural packages or macro-components. For example, the GUI-related components could have been grouped within a higher-level GUI component or package.
Regarding responsibilities, they are currently represented as labels on the associations between components. This makes it possible to understand how responsibilities are distributed across interactions, but I think it would be clearer to place the responsibilities inside the individual component boxes. A component may have multiple responsibilities, whereas the current representation seems to associate each component with only one responsibility.
In this case, the issue may partly derive from the user's request for the "main responsibilities." The model appears to have selected by itself which responsibilities should be considered the main ones, potentially oversimplifying the actual responsibilities of each component.

Folder 1-GR10-Q2:
Apart from some arrows whose source is not clearly defined, i.e., arrows that appear to start from nowhere, the represented elements and the chosen level of granularity seem adequate.

Folder 1-GR10-Q3:
This diagram is neither sufficiently clear nor fully correct. There are UML notation issues, such as the incorrect use of aggregation/composition symbols.
More importantly, the diagram mixes a data-oriented view with a domain-oriented view. For example, there is a relationship between "Segnalazione" and "Cittadino" labeled "id_cittadino." However, "id_cittadino" is a database-level field and should normally appear as an attribute of the corresponding entity/table rather than as the label of a relationship between domain classes.
In addition, the diagram does not show the attributes associated with the different classes or tables. Therefore, a substantial amount of relevant information is missing, making the diagram of limited practical value.
I would have expected a similar class/entity-oriented diagram, but with the relevant fields or attributes explicitly represented inside each entity or class.

Folder 1-GR10-Q4:
Although the diagram does not follow the classical notation of a UML Use Case Diagram, I consider it a good representation in terms of both the information provided and the selected level of granularity.

The proposed representation does not look like a conventional high-level architectural view. The represented elements are not stereotyped using standard architectural or UML concepts such as Component or Class; instead, they are shown as generic boxes.
Moreover, the content inside the boxes mixes architectural element names with descriptions of their functionality. This reduces the distinction between structural elements and behavioral information.
That said, the diagram is still useful for addressing the user's request. However, it does so more as a flowchart-like representation than as a canonical component-based architectural view.

Folder 2-IS-reg-L3:

Folder 2-IS-seat – Overall Comment:
The same general issue observed for Folder 2-IS-reg also appears across the IS-seat diagrams. The elements are represented in a relatively unstructured way, and different abstraction levels are mixed within the same view.
In some cases, low-level details are combined with high-level architectural information, including association labels and even code-level snippets, as in IS-seat-L3: This makes the diagrams difficult to interpret consistently.
On the positive side, the decomposition into higher-level macro-components is generally well performed. However, the overall result remains confusing because the names shown inside the blocks do not always directly correspond to recognizable elements in the source code, while high-level architectural concepts are mixed with implementation-level details.
A possible improvement would be to generate two complementary views instead of forcing all the information into a single diagram: a static view showing the relevant components, layers, or packages, and a dynamic view, such as a sequence diagram, showing their interactions and execution flow.
